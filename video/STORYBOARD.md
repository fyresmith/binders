# Binders preview film: the storyboard, second by second

The cut as shot. 49.3 s, 1920×1080, 60 fps. Track: "Little Alicia", 80 beats a minute; a beat every 0.75 s from
0.73; its last note struck at 47.9 and gone by 49.3. **S** marks the track's strong hits (measured: 0.73, 2.23,
5.23, 6.73, 8.23, 9.73, 14.23, 15.73, 17.23, 20.23, 21.73, 26.23, 27.73, 29.23, 32.23, 33.73, 38.23, 39.73, 41.23,
44.23, 45.73). The times are in `src/direction.mjs`, and `node check.mjs` reads them back with what each move of
the hand had to spare.

## The story

One evening's work on a novel, *The Lighthouse*. Each thing is used because the step before made it needed.
Everything on screen is the real plugin in Obsidian's dark theme, photographed as it was used (`takes.mjs`); nothing
changes but by the pointer, the keys or a finger.

1. Her folder lists its chapters by name. She makes it a binder and puts the Prologue first.
2. Part Two is in the wrong order too. On the corkboard she puts "The wreck" first; the file explorer follows.
3. She reads Part Two as one page. Before she touches "The wreck" she takes a snapshot; she rewrites its last line;
   she looks at what changed. The old words are kept.
4. The scene is done for now: in the outliner she marks it Revised.
5. Later, away from the desk, a doubt. On her phone, the same binder, in tonight's order, the scene marked Revised:
   she adds a revision task to it.
6. Back at the desk the task is waiting in the Revision pane. She goes to it and ticks it off.
7. The draft goes out: Export, a paperback. The click on Export is the cut to everything else Binders does, and
   the name.

## Rules of this cut

- **Nothing is shown that is not used.** No view is arrived at and left.
- **Captions:** nine, six words at most, saying what the writer gets, never the name of a view. Each is set where
  the frame is empty beside the thing it is about. Its words come up from under their line one after another, and a
  second line comes when what it says happens ("The book follows." as the card is dropped); the words that matter
  are underlined as they land. Behind them, only the shadow the letters cast: no plate, no pool. Never two at once,
  none while a line is being typed. Under the last one the wall steps back.
- **The pointer** is drawn by the film: a move takes what its distance and target ask (Fitts), on a slightly bowed
  path, fast early and slow to arrive; it waits on a target before pressing; it is a bar over text and gone while
  keys are typed. On the phone it is a finger's dot.
- **Typing** has a hand's rhythm: uneven, longer before a capital and after a full stop.
- **The camera** moves after its cause, on a spring, and has six places: the explorer; the whole width of the
  window; close on the sentence; close on what changed; the page beside the Revision pane; the wall.
- **The app's own changes play at their own speed**, as photographed.
- **Sound**, under the music (lowered 4 dB from 1.1 s to 41.2 s, 2 dB more under typing): a real mouse's press and
  release and a real keyboard's keys (both recordings in the public domain: `sfx.mjs` names them), kept quiet; and,
  quieter still, a brush as something lifts and a soft tap as it lands, wheel ticks, and air on three moves (5.3,
  27.8, 41.3). A menu makes no sound.

## What changed after the storyboard's review, and why

Four reviewers read the first storyboard second by second (an editor, a story and copy reviewer, a motion and sound
reviewer, and Codex as a second director). Their reports are in `out/review/`.

- **Cut: arranging by label.** Nothing on screen said why "Storm warning" was Mara's scene, and it broke the one
  task the evening has. It is in the wall.
- **Cut: the second drag in the explorer.** The film's book has two parts, so one drag mends the folder.
- **Part Two is opened from the explorer**, one click, in place of opening the binder and then the folder.
- **Added: Revised, in the outliner.** The outliner is used, not visited; and the phone then shows the scene
  marked Revised, which is how the viewer knows it is the same book.
- **The phone has a job and a payoff**, and a caption for the payoff. On the phone the task is added from More
  options with the card selected: the card's own menu has it under a row that is off the foot of the sheet.
- **Typing was given its real time**, and the last key of each line lands on a strong hit.
- **The end:** the camera stops as the name comes; the card is still until the last note; the film is 49.3 s.
- **Not taken up:** Codex would cut the phone and end on the exported file in the system's file manager. The phone
  stays because it is a 1.0 requirement and had never been shown doing anything; the save window and the file
  manager are the system's and cannot be photographed in a headless app.
